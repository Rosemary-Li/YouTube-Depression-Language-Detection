YouTube Depression Signal Detection

Pipeline — share with team

Research questions 1. Across different YouTube content categories, how does the natural prevalence of depression-indicative language vary?
2. To what extent does video-level contextual framing shape the linguistic patterns of depression-related self-disclosure in comments?
Core design principle The analysis unit is the comment, not the video. We do not search for depression-related videos. We sample high-traffic videos across five everyday categories, then scan every comment for depression-indicative language using a three-layer NLP method. Depression relevance is determined entirely post-hoc at the comment level.

Step 2 Comment-Level Depression Signal Detection

Input: comments.json → Output: data/processed/comment_features.csv

Three layers of increasing specificity. Negative sentiment alone is insufficient — "this video is garbage" is negative but not depression-indicative.

Layer 1 Multi-tool sentiment scoring — broad signal
Three independent tools produce a more robust signal than a single model. TextBlob subjectivity (0–1) is particularly useful: high subjectivity correlates with personal self-disclosure.

VADER compound · TextBlob polarity · TextBlob subjectivity · AFINN integer sum
Layer 2 TF-IDF weighted depression lexicon matching — domain-specific
Comments are matched against a LIWC-based mental health lexicon (negative emotion, sadness, anxiety, health) and a depression-specific wordlist. Rather than binary presence/absence matching, TF-IDF weighting with the vocabulary locked to the depression lexicon is applied — capturing how prominently depression terms appear. Extends to bigrams (e.g. "feel hopeless", "can't sleep") for richer coverage.
Layer 3 Self-disclosure pattern detection — high precision
First-person pronoun + depression-indicative vocabulary combinations identified via spaCy POS tagging (e.g. "I feel hopeless", "I've been struggling", "I can't do this anymore"). Captures genuine personal disclosure rather than reactions to video content. Strongest research signal.

Step 3 Cross-Category Comparison & Visualization ★ core result

Input: video_features.csv + comment_features.csv → Output: heatmap.png · tsne.png

Join Step 1 video features with Step 2 comment scores via video_id. Cluster comments, visualise in 2D, and compare depression signal prevalence across the five categories.

Clustering — HDBSCAN
Density-based. No fixed k required. Handles noise naturally. Groups comments by linguistic pattern.
Clustering — GMM
Soft clustering: each comment receives a probability per cluster, not a hard label. Optimal k selected by AIC / BIC. Complements HDBSCAN for ambiguous comments.
Dimensionality reduction
PCA → t-SNE two-step pipeline.
PCA: high-D → 50 components (noise removal).
t-SNE: 50D → 2D (local structure preserved).
Statistical tests
ANOVA + KS-test across categories.

Input: comment_features.csv → Output: data/outputs/model_results.json

Logistic Regression
Strong interpretable baseline. Coefficient analysis shows which features drive signal detection.

Computationally efficient. Well-suited for sparse TF-IDF feature matrices.

Limitations to address in paper
  • Comment ≠ commenter
  • Negative sentiment ≠ depression — addressed by three-layer method
  • Top-comment bias (sorted by likes)
  • Five categories defined by research team — not exhaustive
  • No clinical ground truth
  • Frame results as: "depression-indicative linguistic signals" — not diagnosis
